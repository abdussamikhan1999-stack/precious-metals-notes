# Precious Metals Notes

Notes distilled from a `/pmg/` (Precious Metals General, 4chan-style)
thread — buying/authenticating bullion, numismatics, prospecting, and the
gold-standard/monetary-policy argument behind why the community cares.
Informational only, not financial advice.

See [LINKS.md](LINKS.md) for the full raw link list.

## The "why" behind the hobby: 1971 and the gold standard

**wtfhappenedin1971.com** is the thread's ideological anchor — its thesis
is that Nixon ending the Bretton Woods gold-backed dollar system in 1971
was the inflection point behind wage stagnation, rising inequality, and
asset-price inflation since. Worth flagging clearly: **this is a strongly
opinionated, one-directional argument (essentially "hard money" /
Austrian-economics framing), not a neutral economic consensus** — the
site correlates 1971 with a long list of social/economic trends without
establishing 1971-monetary-policy as the sole or even primary cause of
most of them (many, like wage-productivity decoupling, have competing
mainstream explanations involving globalization, automation, and labor
policy). It's useful for understanding *why* this community is
gold/silver-focused, not as settled economic fact.

## Buying bullion

- **US dealers**: Liberty Coin, Golden Eagle Coin, Stout Gold, Aydin Coins,
  Prospectors Gold and Gems (a longer list in the linked pastebin).
- **UK/EU**: Chards.
- **Specialty**: Silbury Coins (ancient coins specifically), Luciteria
  (rare/exotic metals beyond gold/silver/platinum), MA-Shops (numismatics
  marketplace).
- **Price comparison** — check these before buying from any single dealer,
  since bullion "premium" (price over spot metal value) varies a lot by
  dealer and product: findbullionprices.com (US), eu.compare.pm (EU),
  gold.de's premium table (DE/EU).

## Junk silver — the plain-value entry point

**"Junk silver"** = pre-1965 US dimes/quarters/half-dollars/dollars, which
are 90% silver by content but have *no* collector premium — you're buying
pure metal weight at (ideally) close to spot price, not a numismatic
rarity. War nickels (1942-1945) are the odd one out at 35% silver instead
of the usual 90%. The tradeoff versus buying a modern bullion coin (like a
Silver Eagle): junk silver is usually cheaper per ounce and instantly
recognizable as genuine (mint dates alone narrow it down), but is less
liquid/standardized than a current bullion product for resale.

## Numismatics (coin collecting, as distinct from bullion investing)

- **Numista** and **NGC's World Price Guide** — the two standard
  identification/valuation catalogs for actual collectible coins (where
  rarity/condition/history drive value, not just metal weight).
- **numismag.com** — numismatics-focused news.

## Prices, news, and data

- **SilverSeek**, **SilverDoctors**, **mining.com** — precious-metals-
  specific news/commentary sites (note: this corner of financial media
  skews heavily toward the same "hard money" framing as wtfhappenedin1971;
  read them as advocacy/analysis, not neutral wire reporting).
- **coinflation.com** — calculates a coin's actual melt value from its
  metal content and current spot price (blocked from this environment —
  403, open directly).
- **gold.org/goldhub** (World Gold Council) — the authoritative source for
  central bank gold reserves by country: quarterly data back to 2000,
  updated with a ~2-month lag, sourced from IMF/Fed/World Bank data. Useful
  context: central banks collectively hold roughly a fifth of all gold ever
  mined.

## Authenticating what you buy — the actually load-bearing section

- **fakebullion.com's database** — a crowd-sourced (donor-fed) catalog of
  documented counterfeits with side-by-side photos against genuine
  originals, filterable by brand/metal/weight/year — the single most
  concretely useful resource in this thread if you're buying secondhand or
  from an unfamiliar dealer.
- **Nitric acid test, magnet test, ping test** (the thread's linked videos)
  — standard cheap home-authentication methods: a magnet immediately rules
  out anything ferromagnetic (real gold/silver aren't magnetic), a "ping"
  test checks the ring/resonance of a coin (fakes often sound duller/
  shorter), and a nitric-acid spot test chemically reacts differently with
  base metals than with genuine gold/silver. **None of these are
  individually conclusive** — sophisticated fakes (tungsten-cored gold bars,
  for instance, defeat weight/density checks) are specifically why the
  fakebullion.com database and buying from reputable, established dealers
  matter more than any single home test.

## Prospecting (finding your own, rather than buying)

- **USGS Mineral Resources Program** and Ontario's Ministry of Mines — the
  actual government geological-survey resources for where minerals
  actually occur, as opposed to hobbyist folklore.
- A regional guide (*Gold Creeks and Ghost Towns of British Columbia*) for
  anyone specifically panning/prospecting in that region.

## Other resources

- **coinapps.com** — reference calculators/apps for coin values.
- **erikasgrig.com's RPI/inflation calculator** — historical purchasing-
  power comparison tool (relevant to the thread's broader "money is losing
  value" framing).
- **APMEX's US buying guide** — a mainstream dealer's own onboarding guide
  for first-time US bullion buyers.
